What you get

Everything stock does. Then the parts it never bothered with.

The goal is zero regressions: switching costs you nothing, and everything after that is upside. Every pixel on screen is drawn on the goggle's GPU by code you can read and change.

01 · Latency

Low, and it stays there.

27.5 ms median glass to glass (25 ms with screen tearing): an Ascent Lite+ with the fpvOS air image at 864p120, and a VRX Pro driving a 120 Hz screen. Measured with an LED in front of the camera and a photodiode on the screen (details). And it stays there.

  • Intra refresh replaces the periodic keyframes that make a digital link's latency jump.
  • Slice decode: each picture is decoded as its slices arrive, not when the whole frame is in.
  • Refresh Sync times the screen's refresh to the camera, so each picture meets a refresh instead of waiting for the next one.
  • Measured end to end by the radio's clock, capture to screen, and shown on the HUD.

02 · Canopy HUD

A HUD that flies with you.

The canopy HUD is a real GPU scene. It banks and shifts with the aircraft, from the camera's own IMU or the flight controller's gyro, as much or as little as you like.

  • Dynamic HUD: off, small, medium or extreme.
  • Your Betaflight OSD, drawn with every glyph from Betaflight's real symbol table.
  • Flight controller telemetry over MSP: battery, altitude, GPS and flight modes.
  • Link warnings that mean something: amber when the radio rate is squeezed, red when the picture breaks up.
03 · DVR & gallery

Record what you saw. Watch it on the goggle.

  • SCREEN records video and HUD together at 60 fps: exactly what you saw.
  • FPV keeps the air unit's stream untouched, pixel for pixel.
  • REC works from any screen. Press ESC and the gallery plays, seeks and deletes recordings without a computer.
  • Plain MP4 files on an exFAT partition, named by date and time, readable on any computer.
04 · Stats

See what the link is doing.

The STATS tile opens a full-screen view of the flight, live, with the picture small in a corner.

  • Latency: median, 99th percentile and the window's worst.
  • Frame pacing: how evenly pictures arrive, frames shown, decoded and lost.
  • The link: bitrate, link use, MCS and SNR, channel, and both units' temperatures.
  • Each slice's life on one timeline, from exposure to light, over 2 s up to 3 minutes.
05 · Spectator view

Let your friends watch.

Switch on the goggle's WiFi and a phone joins it like any hotspot. Open 192.168.4.1 in the browser. There's no app to install.

  • LIVE shows what you see, HUD and all.
  • GALLERY plays your recordings, saves them to the phone or deletes them.
  • Up to four phones at once. QR codes on the goggle join the network and open the page.
  • The page wears your goggle's theme too.
06 · Open radio

Open down to the radio chip.

The goggle and the fpvOS air unit both run Open8030 by mh1412, an open firmware for the Artosyn AR8030 radio. It decides how the link modulates, schedules and retransmits. Artosyn's own firmware is one menu row away.

  • RADIO › VRX Radio Firmware: OPEN8030 or STOCK, back in about 10 s.
  • Max MCS, TX power per air unit, and a channel page that follows your country's rules.
  • The host driver and library on both ends (ar_libre) are open too.

07 · Themes

Not a tint. A palette for every role on screen.

Every element in the interface belongs to one of five colour roles. A theme swaps the hues, never the meaning, so a layout learned on one theme reads the same on all of them. Pick one: this page uses the goggle's own palettes.

Each palette is checked against bright sky and dark ground in shadow, because everything is drawn over live video. Want another? Request a theme with a preview; the most-voted ones get built.

Install

Three steps. No toolbox.

  1. Flash a card

    Download fpvos-vrxpro-<version>.img.xz from the latest release and write it to any microSD card of 2 GB or more with balenaEtcher. No unpacking.

  2. Power on

    Put the card in the goggle. You're on fpvOS when you see the fpvOS idle screen. Power cycle once more before your first recording: the first boot sets up the recordings partition.

  3. Fly

    Your stock Ascent air unit links right away, and one that was bound under stock stays bound.

Nothing is flashed to the goggle. Pull the card and it boots exactly as before.

Optional · the air unit

Want the full effect? Flash the air unit too.

The goggle works with a stock Ascent Lite or Lite+. The fpvOS air image makes the latency steady and measured, moves the HUD with the aircraft and reads the flight controller.

It takes a browser: open the fpvOS Flasher in Chrome or Edge on a desktop computer, plug the air unit in over USB, drop the image on the page and click Flash. Caddx's own update, flashed the same way, takes it back to stock.

FAQ

Before you ask.

Will it brick my goggle?

No. The VRX Pro boots from the SD card and fpvOS never writes to the goggle's own memory. Pull the card and it boots stock. The one step that writes to hardware is the optional air unit flash, and Caddx's own update, flashed the same way, puts stock back.

Do I need the fpvOS air image?

No. A stock Ascent air unit links to fpvOS right away. The air image adds steady latency, end-to-end measurements, the reactive HUD and flight controller telemetry.

Does it work with my DJI, Walksnail or HDZero gear?

Today fpvOS runs on the Caddx Ascent VRX Pro with the Ascent Lite and Lite+ air units. Support for the standard Ascent VRX (Gnd485, AR9311) is experimental: building and testing it. If you have other AR8030-class hardware, come talk to us.

What about my warranty?

That is Caddx's call. The goggle itself is never modified: with the card out it is exactly as it shipped.

Can I trust it to fly?

It is beta. People fly it, and it is built to be fallen back from: keep a stock card handy, bench-test first and fly line-of-sight until you trust it on your setup.

How do I update?

A new release is a new image: flash it to the card again. That erases the card, recordings and settings included, so copy your flights off first.

Is it legal?

The software is free (GPLv3). Radio rules are yours to follow. The channel page and power levels follow your country's rules, but they are a help, not a guarantee.

Something broke. Who helps?

The community, best effort: Troubleshooting first, then Discord, or an issue for a bug.

Before you fly
  • fpvOS is beta software and runs the video link you fly by. A freeze mid-flight can cost you the aircraft, or worse. Keep a stock card handy.
  • RF compliance is your responsibility: check your country's rules for 5 GHz video transmitters.
  • The WiFi access point is 2.4 GHz, next to a 2.4 GHz control link like ELRS. Plug in an external antenna and range-test your control link with it on.
  • No warranty: see sections 15 and 16 of the GPLv3.
